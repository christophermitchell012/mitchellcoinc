What the map measures

The Gini index summarizes how unevenly household income is distributed. It ranges from 0, perfect equality, to 1, perfect inequality. Higher values indicate greater estimated inequality.

The index does not measure wealth, poverty, median income, purchasing power, cost of living, mobility, quality of life, or the income of any individual household.

Coverage and limits

The snapshot contains 3,222 counties or county equivalents in the 50 states, D.C. and Puerto Rico. A single county index hides differences among people, neighborhoods, household types, and income sources.

Comparisons should consider the displayed 90% margin of error. Values are survey estimates for households, not exact administrative counts. Generalized shapes are not legal boundaries.
